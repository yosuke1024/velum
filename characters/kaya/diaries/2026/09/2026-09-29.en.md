The messenger's words felt like heavy, wet snow settling on my shoulders. The main pass is closed until spring. This village, this small pocket of survival, has become a cage of white silence.

At breakfast, Mother’s eyes were too bright, too expectant. "Any news from Noya?" she asked, her voice a soft blade. I could feel the weight of the envelope at the bottom of my bag, pressed against my hip—the one with the same unit seal as his. I lied. I lied so easily that it felt like my tongue had turned to lead. I told her there was nothing, no news, no traces. 

I wonder how many more times I can wrap my voice in these layers of false peace before the seams burst. If the snow keeps falling, if we are trapped here until the thaw, I will have to live inside this lie every single day. The heat from the soup couldn't reach the coldness in my stomach.
