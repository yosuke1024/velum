The wind on the Burnt Bridge is always relentless.
Cold air from the north swept down, scattering the ashes at the bottom of the ravine.

I stared at the frayed cuff of Dali’s coat, the white stuffing peeking out like an old arrow wound. He held out two metallic objects. One was a warped brass ring. The other, a canteen with an engraved name.
With these, my bag grows heavier once more.

Back at the station, under the lamp, I examine the ring. Its surface bears fine scratches, as if someone tried to file away a military censorship stamp. I turn the worn pages of the register, tracing the names of those who did not return on that night of torrential rain.

Noya.
My finger passes over the name.

I still cannot write anything to my mother in her letters. Before the snow closes the pass, I must reach the owners of this ring and canteen. Breathing in the faint scent of the heavy uniform resting at the very bottom of my bag, I read out the names again tonight.

Forty-two who did not return.
Until the day I can finally lay down every last burden.

——Indeed, I have delivered them.
